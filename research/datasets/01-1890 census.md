# 1890 Census

## Collected / origin

- The national enumeration put people and households into standardized fields, then faced a processing problem too large for hand tabulation.
- Herman Hollerith's punched-card system converted selected answers into holes that machines could sort and count.
- The 1888 competition timed the approaches; Hollerith's system completed the assigned tabulation in 72.5 hours.

## Why it sticks

- The census is an early data-processing story: a state needs a legible population, then builds machinery to make that population computable.
- Punched cards create a through-line from administrative records to early computing, and eventually to the company that became part of IBM.
- It is also the useful warning that a fast count is not the same thing as a neutral count.

## Caveat in the margin

- Enumerators, not respondents, applied many categories. The 1890 racial classifications were administrative choices with violent historical assumptions, not biological facts.
- Most original population schedules were damaged in the 1921 Commerce Department fire or later destroyed. Published summaries survive, while much individual-level texture does not.
