# Human Genome Project

## Collected / origin

- The project coordinated sequencing, assembly, verification, and release across many laboratories and funders.
- In 1996, the Bermuda Principles argued for rapid public release of large-scale human genomic data.
- The result was not simply a pile of bases. It was a shared reference infrastructure, revised and maintained long after the 2003 milestone.

## Why it sticks

- It makes data sharing part of the scientific story rather than a footnote after publication.
- A reference sequence lets laboratories compare results against a common coordinate system.
- Its scale previewed the modern idea that a dataset can be both an achievement and the platform for thousands of later achievements.

## Caveat in the margin

- A reference genome is not a universal human genome, nor a complete account of human genetic variation.
- Consent, sampling, benefit, and governance remain present even when the final product looks like neutral letters.
