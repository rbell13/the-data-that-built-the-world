# OpenStreetMap

## Collected / origin

- Volunteers map roads, buildings, places, and local detail using surveys, GPS traces, imagery, local knowledge, and edits from other contributors.
- It began in the United Kingdom amid restricted access to conventional map data.
- The collection is never finished. Every edit is both a correction and a new claim about the world.

## Why it sticks

- It turns mapping from a product made by a few institutions into a shared, versioned public project.
- The edit history makes provenance unusually visible: maps are arguments with timestamps and contributors.
- It also demonstrates that a dataset can be an active civic practice rather than a static download.

## Caveat in the margin

- Coverage, contributor knowledge, and update frequency are uneven. Blank space can mean “nothing there,” “nobody mapped it,” or “the data is elsewhere.”
- Reuse has attribution and license obligations, even when export is easy.
