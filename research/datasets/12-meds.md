# MEDS I & II

## Collected / origin

- MEDS I and II are NIST evaluation datasets built from FBI extracts of records of deceased people, prepared by MITRE for NIST.
- Their multiple encounters make it possible to compare the same person across capture conditions and time.
- The material was assembled to measure face-recognition performance and image quality, not to stand in for every face or every camera.

## Why it sticks

- It makes the benchmark question concrete: not “can a model see a face?” but “how does performance change across encounters?”
- Biometrics reveals how much documentation sits behind a seemingly simple image task: source, acquisition, eligibility, access, protocol, and evaluation.
- The dataset turns a portrait into an identification problem, which is precisely why its history matters.

## Caveat in the margin

- Government-collected biometric material carries a dense acquisition and governance history. A research license does not erase that history.
- It is not representative of every person, face, environment, or downstream use.
