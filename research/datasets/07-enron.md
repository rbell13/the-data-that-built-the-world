# Enron Email Corpus

## Collected / origin

- Corporate email gathered during the Enron investigation was later cleaned and released as a research corpus.
- Common descriptions cite roughly 500,000 messages from about 150 mailboxes, but copies and preprocessing histories vary.
- What makes it unusually useful for research also makes it unusually charged: the language is real workplace communication, not fabricated examples.

## Why it sticks

- The corpus supports text analysis, social-network analysis, search, classification, and all the strange things people say at work.
- It is a clean case of administrative and legal records being repurposed into a dataset.
- The archive gives “natural language data” a human scale: threads, signatures, power, routine, and mistakes.

## Caveat in the margin

- Public release is not a magic privacy eraser. Redactions, names, attachments, and quotation boundaries all matter.
- “The Enron dataset” is not one immutable object. Check the source, version, cleaning choices, and whether attachments survive.
